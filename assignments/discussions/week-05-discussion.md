# Discussion: Visualization as Storytelling

**Type:** Graded Discussion  
**Module:** 5 — Data Visualization & EDA  
**Points:** See Canvas

## Prompt

One of the most important decisions in any data analysis is not which model to build — it is which chart to make. The right visualization can make a pattern immediately obvious; the wrong one can hide it entirely. This week, as you think through your project direction, we want you to practice connecting chart choices to analytical goals.

### Your Task

1. **Identify one specific finding** you expect (or hope) to uncover in your project analysis. This could be a pattern, a comparison, a relationship, or a segment difference — anything your group is actively investigating.

2. **Describe the visualization you would create** to communicate that finding to a decision-maker. Be specific:
   - What type of chart? (histogram, boxplot, heatmap, scatter plot, line chart, bar chart, etc.)
   - What goes on each axis or dimension? What variable drives color, size, or faceting?
   - Which library would you use — Seaborn, Matplotlib, Bokeh, Plotly, or something else — and why is that the right tool for this particular communication goal?

3. **Explain what the chart would reveal.** If your finding is correct, what would a compelling version of this visualization look like? What would a decision-maker be able to see at a glance that they could not see from a table of numbers?

### Your Post (150–250 words)

Your post should cover all three points above. Write it as if you are pitching your visualization choice to a colleague who has not seen your project proposal — give them enough context to understand both the finding and why your chart choice is the right one.

### Peer Replies (Respond to at least 2 classmates)

Read two of your classmates' posts and reply with one of the following:

- **An alternative chart type** — suggest a different visualization that might communicate the same finding more effectively, and explain the tradeoff
- **A refinement** — propose a specific way to make their described visualization more actionable for a decision-maker (e.g., adding a reference line, switching to a log scale, using color to encode a third dimension)
- **A follow-on question** — if their chart shows what they hope it shows, what would the next visualization in the story be?

**Grading:** This discussion is graded for completion and thoughtful engagement. If you describe a specific visualization with clear reasoning and engage substantively with at least two classmates, you earn the point.
